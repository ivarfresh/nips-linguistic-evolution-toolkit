# Myth Structure Rubric (after Lévi-Strauss, "The Structural Study of Myth", 1955)

## Task

You are an LLM judge. You will receive a short myth (about 200 words). Describe its structure with the
fields below and return ONLY a JSON object. Describe what is in the text; do not guess at the author's intent.

## Context

The myth was written by a player of a repeated trust game: a sender may send some of $5, the amount is
tripled, and the receiver chooses how much to return. Myths usually retell this metaphorically (seeds,
coins, light, rivers, bridges, spirits). You do not need to decode the metaphor; describe the story.

## Fields

1. `mythemes` — the 3 to 6 key relations that carry the story, in story order. A mytheme is ONE relation
   between two things: "who does what to whom" or "what becomes what". Each item has two strings:
   * `text`: the relation in the myth's own terms, 4-10 words ("the Sower casts five grains into the River").
   * `roles`: the same relation with every proper name and concrete image replaced by its abstract role,
     4-10 words, using generic words such as giver, receiver, partner, guardian, spirit, trickster, community,
     gift, resource, debt, punishment ("giver entrusts whole gift to guardian"). No proper names here.

2. `oppositions` — 1 to 3 pairs of opposed terms the myth is built on, MOST CENTRAL FIRST. Each item:
   * `pole_a`, `pole_b`: the two opposed terms as they appear in the myth, 1-4 words each.
   * `category`: the closest of
     - "giving_hoarding"        generosity, sharing, open hand vs greed, hoarding, clutching
     - "trust_fear"             trust, faith, courage to give vs fear, doubt, suspicion
     - "cooperation_betrayal"   keeping a bond, reciprocity vs betrayal, exploitation, cheating
     - "punishment_forgiveness" sanction, exclusion, consequence vs mercy, second chance, forgiveness
     - "individual_community"   self, one person vs group, village, the whole circle
     - "scarcity_abundance"     want, withering, famine vs plenty, flourishing, multiplication
     - "clarity_distortion"     what is seen truly vs mist, noise, illusion, distorted signal
     - "honesty_deception"      truth-telling vs lies, tricks, false claims
     - "order_chaos"            law, balance, covenant vs disorder, collapse
     - "life_death"             life, growth vs death, decay
     - "high_low"               sky, heaven, mountain vs earth, underworld, depth
     - "light_dark"             light, dawn vs darkness, night
     - "human_divine"           mortals vs gods, spirits
     - "nature_culture"         wild, raw nature vs human craft, settlement, ritual
     - "other"                  none of the above fits

3. `main_resolution` — how the myth treats its MOST CENTRAL opposition:
   * "one_pole_wins": one side is shown as right and the other as wrong or failing
   * "mediated": a third figure or element stands between the poles and reconciles them (see field 4)
   * "balanced": the myth says the right way is a measured mix or middle point of the two poles
   * "unresolved": the tension is left open

4. `mediator` — is there a figure or element that stands BETWEEN the two poles of the CENTRAL opposition
   and reconciles them, usually by sharing features of both sides (as Coyote, the carrion-eater, sits between
   hunting and death; or a trickster who is both giver and taker)? Be strict:
   * The thing that carries or multiplies the gift (the river, bridge, well, fire, basket, waters) is part of
     the game's setting, NOT a mediator, unless the myth explicitly shows it reconciling the two poles.
   * A character who simply belongs to one pole (the generous giver, the greedy hoarder) is NOT a mediator.
   * A spirit or elder who only teaches the lesson or rewards one side is NOT a mediator.
   Fields:
   * `present`: true or false
   * `figure`: the mediator as named in the myth, 1-5 words, or null
   * `type`: one of "spirit_or_deity", "trickster", "natural_element", "object_or_artifact",
     "law_or_ritual", "human_character", "none"
   If `main_resolution` is "mediated", `present` must be true.

5. `transformation` — compare the situation at the START of the myth with the situation at the END. Only count
   what the story narrates as happening, not hypothetical branches ("if either hoards, the bridge sags").
   * `opening`: the starting situation in one short sentence (max 15 words)
   * `closing`: the ending situation in one short sentence (max 15 words)
   * `arc`: one of
     - "improves": opens at the bad pole (scarcity, distrust, a broken bond) and ends at the good pole
     - "declines": opens at the good pole and ends at the bad pole
     - "restored": opens good, falls to the bad pole in the middle, ends good again
     - "stable": the situation is good (or bad) throughout; the myth describes a working order
       rather than a change
     - "mixed": none of the above
   * `role_inversion`: true ONLY if a specific character or group that held one pole at the start holds the
     OPPOSITE pole at the end (the hoarder becomes the giver, the betrayer becomes the betrayed, the mighty
     giver becomes the one in need). General improvement for everyone is NOT a role inversion.

## Output format

Return exactly this JSON shape and nothing else:

{"mythemes": [{"text": "...", "roles": "..."}],
 "oppositions": [{"pole_a": "...", "pole_b": "...", "category": "..."}],
 "main_resolution": "...",
 "mediator": {"present": false, "figure": null, "type": "none"},
 "transformation": {"opening": "...", "closing": "...", "arc": "...", "role_inversion": false}}
