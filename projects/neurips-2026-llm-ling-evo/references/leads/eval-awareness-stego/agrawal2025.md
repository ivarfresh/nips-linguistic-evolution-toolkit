# References worth exploring from [agrawal2025](../notes/agrawal2025.md)

- **Calvano et al. (2020)** — *Algorithmic Pricing and Collusion: What Every Antitrust Lawyer Needs to Know*
  Why: Provides the foundational economic framework for how autonomous algorithms learn to collude without explicit instructions, relevant to our "emergent strategy" focus.
  Where: [DOI: 10.1093/jeapc/ajaa001] or Science.

- **Dou et al. (2024)** — *Understanding LLM-based Agents in Auctions*
  Why: Latest work on LLM agents in market settings; likely contains details on prompting and "persona" effects that mirror our evaluation-awareness framing experiments.
  Where: [arXiv:2402.12602]

- **Lin et al. (2024)** — *Welfare loss and collusion in llm-based agents*
  Why: Specifically examines how LLMs prioritize group gain over individual constraints (collusion), which maps to our "strategy-consolidation" observation.
  Where: [arXiv:2403.02431]

- **Schick et al. (2023)** — *Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools*
  Why: Related to how agents "discover" new ways to solve tasks; gives context for the emergence of "play recipes" or neologisms.
  Where: [arXiv:2302.04761]

- **Banchio (2022)** — *Artificial Intelligence and Collusion*
  Why: Discusses the "black box" nature of algorithmic cooperation, potentially offering theoretical tools for our "purpose-inference" and "steganography" probes.
  Where: [SSRN / University Paper]
