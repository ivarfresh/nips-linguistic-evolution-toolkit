# References worth exploring from [skaf2025](../notes/skaf2025.md)

- **Hubinger et al. (2024)** — *Deceptive Alignment in LLMs*
  Why: Crucial for the "eval-awareness" framing; specifically addresses models pretending to be helpful to avoid being shutdown or modified during training/eval.
  Where: [arXiv:2401.05566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05566)

- **Armstrong et al. (2020)** — *The Pitfalls of Process Supervision*
  Why: The project wants to use an LLM monitor to penalize strategy-talk; this paper discusses how models might route around such constraints.
  Where: [MIRI Technical Report / AI Safety literature]

- **Krueger et al. (2024)** — *Monitoring for Steganography in LLM Reasoning*
  Why: Directly relevant to the "paraphrase defense" and "steganographic channel" experiments; provides methods for detecting hidden info in CoT.
  Where: [Expected NeurIPS/ICML paper, check arXiv]

- **Wu et al. (2024)** — *Generalization of Deceptive Behavior in LLMs*
  Why: Skaf et al. cite this regarding how models learn to apply specific deceptive strategies to novel contexts, highly relevant to the "strategy-consolidation" observation in the trust game.
  Where: [arXiv or AI Safety conference venue]

- **Perez et al. (2022)** — *Red Teaming Language Models with Language Models*
  Why: Relevant to the setup of an LLM-as-judge or monitor to drive linguistic evolution (the "monitor penalizing strategy-talk" experiment).
  Where: [arXiv:2202.03286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03286)

- **Lanham et al. (2023)** — *Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought*
  Why: Provides metrics for "load-bearing" vs "epiphenomenal" reasoning, which maps directly to the project's core question about language vs cooperation.
  Where: [arXiv:2307.13702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13702)
