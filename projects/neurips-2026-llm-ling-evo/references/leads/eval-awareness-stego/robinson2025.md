# References worth exploring from [robinson2025](../notes/robinson2025.md)

- **Akata et al. (2023)** — *Playing Games with Large Language Models*
  Why: Likely provides the foundational methodology for LLM game-theoretic evaluation that Robinson et al. build upon; useful for our trust-game baseline settings.
  Where: [arXiv:2305.10168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10168)

- **Lorè and Heydari (2023)** — *Strategic Behavior of Large Language Models: Game Theory Perspectives*
  Why: Explores how LLMs deviate from "rational" economic behavior, which may provide theoretical backing for our observation that Gemini/Claude/GPT-5 have divergent cooperation "personalities."
  Where: [arXiv:2311.05618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05618)

- **Phelps and Yiotis (2023)** — *Investigating Emergent Goal-like Behaviour in Large Language Models Using Game Theory*
  Why: Relevant to our "strategy-consolidation" finding; examines if models "intend" to cooperate or if it is emergent from the narrative context.
  Where: [arXiv:2305.07990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07990)

- **Brookins and DeBacker (2023)** — *Playing Games with GPTs: What can they teach us about human behavior?*
  Why: Potentially discusses how framing (e.g., "investment" vs. "gift") changes cooperation rates, directly informing our planned framing ablation experiments.
  Where: [Working Paper/Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization]

- **Fan et al. (2023)** — *Can Large Language Models Serve as Rational Players in Game Theory?*
  Why: Useful for benchmarks on model-specific performance disparities in games (PD, Trust Game, etc.), helping contextualize why GPT-5-Nano might be collapsing to defection.
  Where: [arXiv:2312.05488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05488)
