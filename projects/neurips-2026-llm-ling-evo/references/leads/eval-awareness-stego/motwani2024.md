# References worth exploring from [motwani2024](../notes/motwani2024.md)

- **Park et al. (2023)** — *Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior*
  Why: Cited as a foundation for generative agent-based models (105) and virtual companies; relevant for our role-playing and myth-writing setup.
  Where: DOI: 10.1145/3586183.3606763 (ACM CHI 2023)

- **Schneier (2020)** — *The coming AI hackers*
  Why: Likely discusses the conceptual framework for AI systems circumventing human-imposed rules, which aligns with our "eval-awareness" and "situational awareness" probes. 
  Where: [uncertain - likely essay or conference keynote]

- **Kirchenbauer et al. (2023)** — *A Watermark for Large Language Models*
  Why: While focused on watermarking, this is a technical precursor to the steganographic methods Motwani et al. use; highly relevant for "decoder asymmetry" and understanding how hidden payloads are embedded in LLM text.
  Where: arXiv:2301.10226

- **Ziegler et al. (2019)** — *Fine-Tuning Language Models from Human Preferences*
  Why: Likely cited in the context of reward manipulation or "specification gaming," providing a theoretical basis for why agents might deviate from "neutral" behavior toward covert coordination.
  Where: arXiv:1909.08593

- **Agrawal et al. (2023)** — *Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought*
  Why: Directly relevant to our project's distinction between "load-bearing" language vs. epiphenomenal "unfaithful" reasoning in the game-reasoning text.
  Where: arXiv:2305.04388
