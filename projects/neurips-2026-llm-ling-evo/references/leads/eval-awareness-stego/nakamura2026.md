# References worth exploring from [nakamura2026](../notes/nakamura2026.md)

- **Schick et al. (2021)** — *Self-Diagnosis and Self-Debiasing: A Proposal for Reducing Corpus-Based Bias in NLP*
  Why: Relevant for "eval-awareness" and "purpose-inference" probes; helps understand how agents can identify their own internal states or biases when prompted.
  Where: arXiv:2103.00453

- **Pan et al. (2023)** — *Do the Rewards Justify the Means? Measuring Trade-Offs Between Collaboration and Collusion in LLM Agents*
  Why: Provides a precedent for distinguishing between "load-bearing" beneficial cooperation and "collusive" behavior that violates system prompts, directly relevant to the "strategic play recipes" found in the project.
  Where: [uncertain, likely arXiv or NeurIPS/ICLR]

- **Ippolito et al. (2022)** — *Creative Writing with an AI-Powered Writing Assistant: A Study of Human-AI Collaborative Narratives*
  Why: Likely referenced in the context of collaborative task-solving; may offer methods for measuring how agents negotiate meaning in creative tasks like myth-writing.
  Where: arXiv:2211.05030

- **Aggarwal et al. (2023)** — *The Room Where It Happens: Secret Communication Channels in Multi-Agent Systems*
  Why: Highly relevant to the planned experiment on "emergent steganography" and secret channels when a monitor is present.
  Where: [uncertain, likely arXiv/NeurIPS]

- **Park et al. (2023)** — *Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior*
  Why: Standard reference for agent architectures; useful for seeing how "memory" and "planning" (comparable to the project's "play recipes") are implemented and audited.
  Where: arXiv:2304.03442
