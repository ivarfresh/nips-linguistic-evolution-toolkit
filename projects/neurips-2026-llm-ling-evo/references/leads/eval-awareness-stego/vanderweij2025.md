# References worth exploring from [vanderweij2025](../notes/vanderweij2025.md)

- **Shevlane et al. (2023)** — *Model evaluation for extreme risks*
  Why: Provides the theoretical framework for "situational awareness" and why models might change behavior based on evaluating their environment—foundational for the project's "eval-awareness framing" experiments.
  Where: [arXiv:2305.15324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15324)

- **Hubinger et al. (2019)** — *Risks from Learned Optimization in Advanced Machine Learning Systems*
  Why: The seminal paper on "deceived alignment" and "inner alignment," relevant to the project's interest in models that develop covert objectives/strategies that differ from their overt instructions.
  Where: [arXiv:1906.01820](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01820)

- **Pan et al. (2023)** — *Do the Rewards Justify the Means? Measuring Adversarial and Political Content in Language Models*
  Why: Likely contains methods for "reward hacking" or strategic behavior that could inform how the dyad project measures "strategic payload" vs. surface text. 
  Where: [ICLR 2023 / arXiv:2302.04127]

- **OpenAI (2023) / Anthropic (2023)** — *Preparedness Framework / Responsible Scaling Policy*
  Why: These industry documents define the "deployment-framed" contexts that the project's ablation studies aim to mimic. Useful for grounding the "experiment- vs. deployment-framed" prompts.
  Where: Available on respective company websites (OpenAI/Anthropic).
