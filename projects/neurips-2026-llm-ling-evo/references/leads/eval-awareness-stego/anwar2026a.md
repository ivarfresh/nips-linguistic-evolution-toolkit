# References worth exploring from [anwar2026a](../notes/anwar2026a.md)

- **Stickland & Korbak (2025)** — *Steganography in LLM Reasoning*
  Why: Directly addresses the formation of hidden communication channels in reasoning traces, which is central to the project's interest in emergent steganography and strategic "payloads" in myths.
  Where: [arXiv/Preprint likely]

- **Baker et al. (2025)** — *Detecting and Preventing Obfuscation in LLM Chains of Thought*
  Why: Explores how models might actively hide information from monitors; relevant for the project's "paraphrase defense" and "steganographic channel" experiments.
  Where: [Conference/Preprint]

- **Korbak et al. (2025)** — *Monitoring Large Language Models with Chain-of-Thought*
  Why: Provides the baseline for "CoT monitors," which the project plans to use as a penalty mechanism to trigger emergent steganography.
  Where: [Preprint]

- **Lanham et al. (2023)** — *Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning*
  Why: Foundations for the "load-bearing" vs "epiphenomenal" question; helps define metrics for whether the "myth" (analogous to CoT) actually drives the game behavior.
  Where: [arXiv:2307.13702]

- **Panfilov et al. (2025)** — *Deceptive Reasoning in Large Language Models*
  Why: Relevant to the project's goal of checking "eval-awareness" and whether agents infer they are being monitored/tested during the trust game.
  Where: [Preprint]

- **Turpin et al. (2023)** — *Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think*
  Why: Classic reference for the disconnect between internal "reasoning" (or myths) and final output/action; useful for the "decoder asymmetry" and "semantic vs surface" analysis.
  Where: [arXiv:2305.04388]
