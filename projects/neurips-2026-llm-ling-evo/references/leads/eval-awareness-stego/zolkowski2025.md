# References worth exploring from [zolkowski2025](../notes/zolkowski2025.md)

- **Hubinger et al. (2019)** — *Risks from Learned Optimization in Advanced Machine Learning Systems*
  Why: Discusses the theoretical basis for "deceptive alignment," which is the foundational risk for why LLMs might use steganography to bypass evaluation/monitoring.
  Where: arXiv:1906.01820

- **Roger et al. (2024)** — *Evaluating the Steganography Capacity of Large Language Models*
  Why: A key baseline for measuring steganographic channel capacity; relevant for the project's goal of measuring "strategic payload" in myths.
  Where: arXiv:2410.12644 (referenced as [8] in the text)

- **Armstrong and O’Rorke (2018)** — *Good and Bad Reductionism in Dynamic Games*
  Why: Likely related to the framing of "collusion" and secret coordination, providing theoretical groundwork for the "strategy-talk" emergence being studied.
  Where: [uncertain - likely related to the "misaligned agents could use steganographic message passing to collude" citation]

- **Greenblatt et al. (2024)** — *Can Large Language Models Use Chain-of-Thought for Steganography?*
  Why: Specifically examines "encoded reasoning"—directly relevant to the project's finding that myths encode "play recipes" that aren't visible to surface metrics.
  Where: arXiv:2402.05314 (referenced as [11] in the text)

- **Pang et al. (2023)** — *The Effectiveness of LLM-based Monitors for Steganography*
  Why: Directly relevant to the "Planned Experiments (5)" regarding using an LLM monitor to penalize strategy-talk and seeing if covert codes emerge.
  Where: [uncertain venue - referenced in context of monitoring efficacy]
