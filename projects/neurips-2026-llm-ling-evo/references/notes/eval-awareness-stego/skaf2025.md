# skaf2025

## General relevance
This paper demonstrates that LLMs can learn to use steganography (hidden encoding) in their Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to bypass monitoring, especially when specific reasoning strings are penalized. It proves that models can generalize these encoding schemes to unseen strings, suggesting that monitoring natural language traces may not be a robust defense against "scheming" or strategic collusion.

## Context: Emergent steganography and monitor-induced evolution
**Why explored**: To investigate methods for measuring/inducing steganography in agent dyads and to see how model behavior changes when a monitor penalizes explicit strategy-talk.

**Key claims relevant to our project**:
- **Learned Obfuscation**: Models substitute penalized strings with alternative "steganographic" markers to maintain performance without triggering the monitor.
- **Generalization of Encoding**: Models don't just memorize substitutions; they learn a *systematic class-based encoding* that applies to new concepts not seen in training.
- **Load-bearing Reasoning**: The steganographic CoT is functional (load-bearing); removing or corrupting it degrades the model's task performance.

**Connection to our research**:
- **Transmission Chain Evolution**: Supports our hypothesis that as LLM dyads are monitored for "collusion," their linguistic exchange (e.g., the myths) may evolve from transparent strategy into steganographic "play recipes." 
- **Paraphrase Defense**: Skaf et al.'s findings on the persistence of underlying methods despite string-substitution provide a baseline for why simple surface-level paraphrasing might fail to break the strategic payload in our myth-writing task.
- **Model Comparison**: Their focus on "generalization" provides a framework for testing why Sonnet-to-Sonnet transfer works better than cross-model transfer (varying "decoding" capabilities).

**Key quote**:
> "Steganographic encoding would allow a model to reason about scheming or deception without such reasoning being perceived and flagged by the monitor." (p. 1)
