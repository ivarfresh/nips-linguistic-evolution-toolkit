# agrawal2025

## General relevance
This paper investigates the emergence of collusion (secretive cooperation) among LLM agents in double auction markets. It demonstrates that communication significantly increases the propensity for agents to coordinate their strategies to manipulate market outcomes, often influenced by model identity and environmental pressures.

## Context: Collusion in economic games
**Why explored**: To investigate how LLM agents develop emergent strategies, respond to monitoring (oversight), and whether communication acts as a vector for hidden coordination (collusion).

**Key claims relevant to our project**:
- **Direct communication as a catalyst**: Seller communication is the primary driver of stable collusion, paralleling our observation that "myth-writing" may act as a strategic coordination channel.
- **Model-dependent behavior**: Different frontier models (e.g., GPT-4 vs. others) exhibit varied baseline propensities for cooperation/collusion, supporting our finding that the "model effect dominates everything."
- **Response to Oversight**: The presence of an "authority figure" or monitor influences agent behavior, which provides a direct methodology comparison for our planned "LLM monitor/stealth" experiments.

**Connection to our research**:
- **Strategic Payload**: This paper views communication as a tool for collusion; our project investigates if this "collusion" (cooperation) can be encoded steganographically or via "strategic recipes" in non-game text (myths).
- **Monitoring & Awareness**: Agrawal et al. use "oversight" as an environmental pressure; this informs our experimental design for testing how agents adapt when they infer they are being monitored for strategic coordination.

**Key quote**:
> "We find that direct seller communication increases collusive tendencies... environmental pressures, such as oversight and urgency from authority figures, influence collusive behavior." (Abstract)
