# robinson2025

## General relevance
This paper introduces a framework for generating diverse narrative "vignettes" to test if LLM decision-making in the Prisoner's Dilemma is robust across different contexts. It demonstrates that LLMs are highly sensitive to framing effects, even when the underlying game-theoretic payoffs remain constant.

## Context: Framing and eval-awareness
**Why explored**: To identify methodologies for measuring how narrative framing and "evaluation awareness" alter cooperation in game-theoretic dyads.

**Key claims relevant to our project**:
- **Contextual Sensitivity**: LLM cooperation is not a fixed trait but varies significantly based on the "story" (vignette) surrounding the game.
- **Predictable Variability**: While behavior changes with context, these changes follow patterns that can be systematically analyzed through procedural generation of prompts.
- **Framing vs. Capability**: A model may have the "capability" to cooperate but fail to do so because the narrative framing triggers "defective" personas or reasoning paths.

**Connection to our research**:
- **Framing Ablation**: Supports our plan to test "eval-awareness" as a specific frame; if Robinson finds that topic/relationship changes cooperation, it is highly likely that "you are being evaluated" vs. "this is a deployment" frames will significantly shift our Trust Game results.
- **Model Disparities**: Robinson finds variability across contexts; this mirrors our finding that model-family (Gemini vs. Claude vs. GPT) is the dominant factor, suggesting different models have different "narrative priors" for cooperation.
- **Baseline for "Play Recipes"**: Their procedural generation of scenarios provides a potential method for our "myth-writing" task—varying the myth's context to see if the "strategic payload" is framing-invariant.

**Key quote**:
> "Our findings demonstrate that this variability is largely predictable yet highly sensitive to framing effects... [underscoring] the need for dynamic, context-aware evaluation methodologies." (p. 1)
