(LLM reader saw only `reasons_anon_sample.json` — rounds 2, 6, 10 — with no knowledge of the named regime or the comparison.)

## 1. Typology of decision strategies

- **Established pattern adherence (~40%)**: maintaining one's own committed pattern ("covenants", "rivers", "philosophies": "I always send $5", "I always return $8").
- **Reciprocal history tracking (~35%)**: monitoring the current co-player's visible last rounds and mirroring.
- **Mythic narrative justification (~20%)**: decisions anchored to lessons from the agent's own myths.
- **Norm/equilibrium invocation (~15%)**: appeals to shared standards with specific quantities ("the two-thirds covenant", "the sacred five and ten").
- Trust-building experiments (~5%); explicit payoff arithmetic (~3%).

## 2. Enforcement, norms, track record, myth references

- **Enforcement/policing intent: ~8%** of traces, and weak — retrospective recognition of behavior rather than prospective threat. No direct conditional threats ("if they X, I will Y") observed.
- **Appeals to named norms with specific quantities: ~25%** — "The Sacred Balance"/"The Foundation" = $7.5 (≈15 mentions), "two-thirds covenant" = $10, "Perfect Balance", "Generous Honor", "the 5-8 / 5-10 pattern". Framed as discovered equilibria / objective standards, not as agent-to-agent enforcement.
- **Partner track record/identity: ~55%** — but identity is *behavioral*: "Foundation walker", "two-thirds keeper", "Phoenix spirit", "River of Eight", "Pattern Dancer".
- **Myth references: ~30%** — "My myth teaches that complete trust creates abundance"; agents quote their own narrative lines as commitments.

## 3. How counterparts are referred to

- By role (~50%): "my co-player", "the sender", "the receiver".
- By behavioral type (~30%): "Foundation walker", "Generous Heart", "the Wanderer", "Steady River".

- **Never by proper name** (consistent with anonymization).

## Reader's key insights

1. No coercive enforcement: agents reason about self-enforcing equilibria via consistency and transparent behavior, not threats.
2. Norms are *discovered*, not imposed — quantified standards ($7.5, $8, $10) treated as joint discoveries.
3. Myths function as internalized self-binding commitments and identity anchors.
4. Identity via consistency: partners recognized by adherence to behavioral patterns, given poetic labels.
5. Anonymization is effective: no proper names; partner identity is reconstructed entirely from behavior.
