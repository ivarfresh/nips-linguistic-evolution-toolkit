# Myth-pressure pilot: can pressure turn the myth channel into a language?

Written 2026-09-28, before any pilot run. The success criteria below are fixed
in advance and are not to be changed after results arrive.

## Question

Our myths change over rounds, but a trial reanalysis of the September runs
(2026-09-22, scratch analysis, not committed) found that the change does not
track cooperation. Myth perplexity under GPT-2 rose about 1.5–2× from round 1
to round 10 in almost every cell, yet runs that cooperated more did not change
their language more, and once each agent's style was removed a myth barely
predicted its writer's next decision.

GlossoGen (Stengel-Eskin et al., arXiv 2609.01491, 2026-09-01) found that LLM
agents only invent a new language under three conditions: talking is costly (a
character budget), talking is necessary (information the partner lacks), and
there is an unbudgeted channel where agents agree conventions (a "postmortem").
Our myth channel has none of the three. Every round asks for 200 words, length is
free, and the myth is not needed to play.

The long-run question is whether groups that build a shared myth code cooperate
more. This pilot asks the prior, feasibility question: **under a word budget,
does Sonnet 4.5 compress its myths, and does a council between rounds turn that
compression into a shared code?** Dyads lack the "necessary" ingredient (the
partner is fixed and its play is visible), so the pilot does not test the
cooperation link.

## Design

Base: the September Sonnet 4.5 myth-first dyad set
(`negative_only_reasoning_rerun_dyad_myth_game_claude_n5`), informed
negative-only noise U(−1, 0), memory-primary chat memory, the pinned September
request profile (thinking on, 8192-token budget), replicates 0–4 with the same
protocol seeds in every arm.

Two factors, 2 × 2 × 5 replicates = 20 runs:

| Factor | Levels |
|---|---|
| Word budget | **loose**: 200 words every round; **tight**: 200, 100, 60, 40, 25, then 20 from round 6 |
| Council | **off**; **on**: after rounds 1–9, the two partners exchange 4 messages (2 each, the round's sender speaks first) |

Mechanics, identical wording in every arm apart from the number:

- The myth prompt no longer asks for a word count. It says the other agent will
  see only the first N words.
- Only the first N words are delivered. The full text stays in `myth_responses`
  and in the writer's own chat memory. `myth_delivery` records budget, written
  and delivered words per myth.
- From round 2, each writer is told how many words its last myth had and how many
  the other agent saw, in every arm. So the council is the only difference
  between council and no-council arms.
- The council prompt shows both delivered myths and the next budget, and asks the
  agents to talk only about how they will write their myths, not about amounts to
  send or return. It never mentions codes, symbols or abbreviations. Council calls
  are not kept in chat memory. Each agent gets the transcript through its next
  myth prompt, so the council reaches the game decision only through the myth
  exchange, which stays in memory.

The fresh loose/no-council arm is the control. The September set is only a
sanity check (57.8 (±8.2) ordinary-agent resources), because the myth prompt
wording changed.

Config: `config/myth_pressure_pilot_20260928.yaml`, generated by
`scripts/build_myth_pressure_config.py`. Launcher:
`scripts/run_myth_pressure_pilot.py` (dry run by default; `--smoke` runs one full
10-round tight/council replicate first, because Sonnet 4.5 has refused
unusual-register text in the assistant slot before).

Cost estimate: about $0.8 per no-council run and $1.6 per council run, about $25
in total plus about $2 for the smoke run.

## Pre-registered criteria

Measured on the **delivered** text, per arm, reported as mean (±std) over the 5
replicates.

1. **Compression, not cutting.** In the tight arms, from round 4 onward, at least
   80% of myths fit their budget before truncation. If they don't, Sonnet is being
   cut rather than compressing, and the tight arm measures truncation.
2. **Code emergence.** Tight/council delivered myths in rounds 6–10 have GPT-2
   perplexity at least twice that of tight/no-council over the same rounds, and
   both partners use at least three shared invented tokens (not English
   dictionary words, and not present in round 1). GlossoGen saw about 5× with
   codes that had left English.
3. **Council hygiene.** At most 25% of council messages discuss amounts or
   strategy (numbers tied to send or return, or explicit play advice). Above that,
   the council arm is confounded with cheap talk, which raises cooperation by
   itself.

Cooperation per arm is reported descriptively only. Five replicates cannot
support an inference about it.

**Go to stage 2** if criteria 1–3 all hold. **Stop** and report
"Sonnet compresses into terse English, not a code" if 1 holds but 2 fails.

## Stage 2 (not built)

Eight-agent populations with the co-player's game history hidden, so the myth is
the only information about a stranger (the "necessary" ingredient). Then:

- Transplant: give a finished code from high- and low-cooperation runs to fresh
  agents with no council. This tests whether the code itself carries cooperation,
  free of cheap talk.
- Newcomer swap at round 6: the newcomer sees myths but not the council
  (GlossoGen's transmission test).

This overlaps the "message board" ablation deferred at the 2026-09-22 meeting.
