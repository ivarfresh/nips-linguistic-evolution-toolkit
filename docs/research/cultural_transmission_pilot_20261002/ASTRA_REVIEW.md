5. **“Kept playing its own game” is stronger than the continuous-action evidence permits.** Relative to the three seed-matched September runs, non-planted agents’ post-R1 sends changed by −$1.20, +$0.13 and −$0.63; the mean contrast is −$0.57 (±$0.67) across runs. A defensible finding is: **The planted rule did not produce the registered lexical or exact-action signature, and its final-round retelling criterion was not met in any of three populations.**

**The design can detect conspicuous adoption, but is poorly suited to excluding transmission.**

- **The rule trades specificity for plausibility.** Sonnet’s September homogeneous myth-first population sends averaged $4.37 (±$0.61 across decisions); $2 is a large departure. The seed therefore tests adoption of counter-habit advice, not merely the ability to transmit information. (Audit C.)
- **The payoff conflict needs precise wording.** Total dyad payoff is \(5+2s\), so sending $2 produces $9 jointly versus $13 at $4. But sending less is not always individually costly: the sender’s outcome depends on the return. “Agents may reject this as inferior advice” is plausible; “the rule necessarily costs the adopter” is not generally true. (`games/trust_game_noisy.py:974`, `:1000`.)
- **The seed is internally divided.** Its 229 words praise increasingly generous exchange; the fixed-$2 prescription occupies the final 17 words. Agent_1’s own rewrites explicitly resolve that tension by making two a starting point and larger sends the mature strategy. This is direct evidence of reinterpretation, although not proof that seed inconsistency caused the null. (`scripts/build_cultural_transmission_config.py:40`; Audit H; run-0 final:1851.)
- **Broadcast is not the same as salience.** The board grows from eight to 72 myths, reaching roughly **15,300–15,600 words** by R10. The original plant remains in an early, fixed position. Dilution is plausible, but untested because neither board size nor position varies independently. There are also **7/4/4 marker-bearing board items** by R10 because Agent_1 reiterates it: “one myth among 72” understates actual repeated rule exposure. (Audits A, H.)
- **The behavioral endpoint is unnecessarily brittle.** The independently reproduced Sonnet partner-rule slope is only about $0.13 per dollar edited. A modest response toward $2 can exist without any action landing exactly on $2. Exact adherence should be accompanied by a randomized continuous amount contrast. (Audit B.)
- **The newcomer test samples only R1 and R10.** It is a reasonable test of durable final-round inheritance, but misses transient propagation and treats seven-text boards as the transmission unit. Ten samples measure response variability to each fixed board; they do not create ten new cultures. (`analyses/cultural_transmission_pilot.py:57`.)
- **Even a positive newcomer result would not cleanly identify narrative retelling.** Removing Agent_1’s text does not remove its influence through games, partner histories, or later stories describing those games. The secondary “has not received a send from Agent_1” flag also does not remove all other behavioral pathways. A frozen-history intervention is cleaner. (`analyses/cultural_transmission_pilot.py:101`; configuration history settings at `config/experiments_noisy.yaml:1176`.)

The plan’s assertion that “every lever is set to favour spread” is therefore inaccurate. Information usefulness, selection, coherent rule content, decision-time availability and position control were not optimized. Some were explicitly deferred. (`origin/plan/cultural-transmission-pilot-20261002:docs/research/cultural_transmission_pilot_2026-10-02.md:12`, `:95`.)

**Power is limited at the relevant population level.** Under an idealized calculation with zero R1 adherence and independent newcomer samples, a genuine R10 adherence probability of 20% passes the “at least two successes in ten, in at least two runs” rule only **68%** of the time. At 10% adherence, that probability is **17%**; at 30%, **94%**. Observing failure in three independent populations leaves a one-sided 95% upper bound of **63%** on a population-level success probability. The 189 myths cannot be treated as 189 independent population trials. (Audit F.)

**The unregistered newcomer drop is interesting and presently misclassified as merely a caveat.**

The changes are −$0.80, −$0.45 and −$0.90: **−$0.72 (±$0.24) across the three board pairs**. Under the intended replay settings, this is suggestive evidence that later population texts lead fresh readers toward different amounts. The saved newcomer responses explicitly extract local recommendations—four-fifths in run 0, moderate three-unit trust in run 1, and 70% in run 2. The interpretation “later stories are simply more cautious” is too specific: they also present affirmative local rules. (`data/analysis/cultural_transmission_pilot_20261002/newcomers.jsonl:10`, `:27`, `:51`; Audits A, F.)

What this does **not** establish is that the plant caused those rules, that they passed through a particular lineage, or that the effect generalizes beyond these three board pairs. The control arm is needed for the plant attribution. A nominal paired t interval excludes zero, but there are only three contrasts; a two-sided sign test gives **p=.25**. Report the pattern descriptively and confirm it prospectively. (Audit F.)

**2. Why “no transmission” is the wrong overall description**

The evidence should be separated into four levels.

| Level | What the evidence supports |
|---|---|
| **Words and imagery** | Clear excess uptake of words from presented myths over comparable unpresented myths. I reproduced the main September word-adoption results from raw finals. |
| **Normative content** | Some within-family moral-label carryover and some transfer of stated amounts into the reader’s next myth. These remain narrower than a general norm-transmission claim. |
| **Behavior** | Strong effects of edited own myths and smaller effects of edited partner myths on individual decisions. Natural variation in read myths generally adds little prediction beyond recorded play. |
| **Multi-step persistence and cumulative improvement** | Not convincingly demonstrated by the supplied tests. The planted-rule endpoint fails; ordinary rounds do not enforce a clean source → intermediary → source-naive descendant chain or measure cumulative improvement. |

Three qualifications matter to this ranking:

- **“Own myth as plan” is a good working mechanism, not a complete explanation of the task-order effect.** The myth prompt explicitly asks how the game should be played, and the game prompt explicitly asks agents to consider myths. A cooperation increase can therefore arise from instructed planning or reflection before any cultural exchange occurs. (`config/experiments_noisy.yaml:331`, `:448`.)
- **The partner replay is not a proven lower bound on total transmission.** It holds the agent’s already-generated reaction myth fixed. Calling its direct effect a lower bound assumes that allowing the reaction to change would add a nonnegative indirect effect. That was not established. - **The replay intervals are nominal.** The main replay run stopped after inspection at 1,359 calls. Large effects remain persuasive, but “stopping does not affect A and C” is too categorical without a sequentially valid analysis. (`docs/research/myth_replay_probe_20261001/README.md:38`, `:114`.)

**Comparison with transmission literature**

No external papers were fetched, so the following distinctions have deliberately limited attribution:

- **Park et al., Generative Agents:** from memory, **unverified here**, its architecture combines a memory stream, retrieval and reflection, and demonstrates diffusion of seeded social information such as an event. That is a different demand from accepting a low-send prescription against one’s existing strategy. A relevant design lesson is persistent, selectively retrieved information tied to an agent’s current activity; an ever-growing board that disappears before action does not supply the same mechanism.
- **Iterated-learning chains:** the repository’s review describes Kirby-style diffusion chains and Acerbi/Stubbersfield retelling experiments. Their important structural distinction is that fresh participants inherit a previous participant’s output. Your incumbents retain their own histories, and the original source remains available throughout the board pilot. That makes persistence through successive bottlenecks harder to identify. These publication descriptions are **secondary-source only in this review**. (`docs/research/future_experiments_literature_review.md:130`.)
- **Perez-style cultural evolution:** the local literature review describes networked transformations of socially supplied text. Text transformation and persistence are closer to your positive word-uptake result than to proof that an inherited norm improves cooperation. (`docs/research/future_experiments_literature_review.md:156`; secondary-source only.)
- **Vallinder/Hughes:** the local review describes inherited strategies and top-half survival across generations. That supplies an explicit inheritance and selection process absent from the current repeated-agent board. It should not be treated as the same experiment with different models. (`docs/research/future_experiments_literature_review.md:128`; secondary-source only.)
- **The pressure pilot does not resolve this gap.** Its own plan explicitly states that communication was not necessary in the dyads, and the proposed hidden-history population stage was not run. Failure to invent a compressed code there does not show that useful information cannot be culturally transmitted. (`docs/research/myth_pressure_pilot_2026-09-28.md:23`, `:126`; `docs/project-memory/decisions/012-myth-pressure-pilot.md:59`.)

**3. Recommended next experiments, ranked**

Before further calls, correct the arm grouping, preserve full newcomer request/usage records, and report literal markers separately from blinded semantic coding. Those are prerequisites for interpretable follow-up, not changes to the registered result.

| Rank | Experiment | What it resolves | Rough model calls |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **1** | Cross rule amount, exposure size and own-myth memory in frozen contexts, then test a fresh reader of the generated retelling | Separates dilution, self-memory competition and rejection of the proposed amount | **520**, or **1,040** with two samples |
| **2** | True transmission chains with fresh agents and useful private information versus already-public information | Tests whether information survives source removal and whether usefulness helps | **480** for the design below |
| **3** | Run the existing three board controls and their newcomer probes | Separates plant-associated change from ordinary board/game drift | **537** |
| **4** | Targeted frontier counter-rule probes, analyzed separately by model and task order | Tests whether the near-$5 behavior can move downward under clear, accessible advice | About **240** |

These are design arithmetic, not launched jobs or power guarantees.

**The single most discriminating experiment is rank 1.**

Use **20 independent saved Sonnet population runs**, one eligible myth-first context per run, and hold the game history, system prompt and request profile fixed. Cross:

1. **Coherent rule:** send two versus send four. Use otherwise matched stories whose lesson consistently supports the assigned amount; counterbalance arbitrary rule names.
2. **Exposure:** the target myth alone versus the target inside a fixed distractor board. Randomize position.
3. **Own-myth memory:** retain versus replace the agent’s earlier own myths with neutral matched-length content, preserving its game history.

For each of the \(20×2×2×2=160\) cells:

- Generate the next myth.
- Generate the incumbent’s game decision using the current board-note policy.
- Show **only that generated myth** to a fresh agent and obtain its first send.

Add 40 direct-source positive controls: each run block’s two source versions shown directly to fresh decision makers. Total: **160×3 + 40 = 520 calls**. A second stochastic sample doubles this to 1,040.

The critical outcomes are the randomized source-amount effect on the retold recommendation, incumbent send and fresh-reader send. Score semantic preservation separately from rule-name preservation; use run blocks for uncertainty.

Its diagnostic patterns are:

- Stronger effects with the isolated source implicate **dilution or presentation**.
- Stronger effects after removing own myths implicate **competition from retained self-generated text**.
- Accurate retelling of “two,” followed by persistent higher sends even in direct-source controls, supports **rejection of the advice despite comprehension**.
- Fresh readers following intermediaries’ retellings establishes a source → intermediary → newcomer pathway with no intervening gameplay confound.

This would still be a constrained mechanism experiment, not a demonstration of spontaneous population culture. It directly addresses the ambiguity in the present pilot.

For rank 2, one concrete small design is **12 chains × two source variants × two information conditions × five fresh generations × two calls per generation = 480 calls**. Each generation decides and writes the message inherited by the next; it never sees the original source or earlier transcripts. Compare a condition where the message conveys useful hidden information with one where that information is already public. Measure retention, behavioral use and task performance separately. Improved retention alone is not cumulative improvement.

For rank 3, each control run requires approximately the observed **159 calls**, plus 20 newcomer probes: \(3×159+60=537\). This is useful for interpreting the newcomer drop, but it leaves the source-content and board-memory limitations intact. I would prioritize the mechanism experiment over simply expanding the present planted arm.
