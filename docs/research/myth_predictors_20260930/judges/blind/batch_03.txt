=== M065
**The Myth of the River and the Bridge**

In ancient times, two villages stood on opposite banks of a great river. Each village possessed different treasures—one had abundant grain, the other rich fish. Yet the river ran too swift and deep for either to cross alone.

One day, a villager from the Grain Shore gathered stones and threw them into the water—not all his stones, for he feared losing everything, but enough to show good faith. The River Spirit, seeing this offering, multiplied the stones threefold, creating stepping stones that reached partway across.

A villager from the Fish Shore saw these stones and understood the gesture. She added her own stones to continue the path, and again the River Spirit tripled them. Back and forth they built, each contributing measured amounts, neither giving so much they'd be left vulnerable, nor so little that progress would stall.

Those who threw all their stones at once drowned in presumption. Those who threw none starved in isolation. But those who gave and received in balance built a bridge that endured generations.

The River Spirit whispered: "Trust is not blind faith—it is the careful dance of giving and returning, where both shores must contribute for the crossing to complete."

=== N024

Thus the sacred law was carved into the living rock: give your entire measure without fear, divide the threefold bounty in perfect balance, and let steadfast honor outshine every passing veil of mist.

=== N030
Myth: In ancient times, two villages stood on opposite sides of a barren valley. A new Guardian of Growth arrived and chose courage over fear. "I cannot control what returns," she said, "but I can control what I give." She poured half her vessel into the valley.

The water transformed—tripling as it reached the Guardian of Sustenance. He stood holding abundance beyond his dreams, a gift born from another's faith. The Guardian of Growth watched from across the valley, waiting to see if her risk would be honored.

The Guardian of Sustenance smiled—and poured nothing back. He kept every drop, his well overflowing while hers ran dry.

The next season arrived. The Guardian of Growth stood at the valley's edge again, but this time she poured nothing. "Trust must flow both ways," she said. "I offered the gift of faith, and you returned only betrayal. The valley's magic requires both guardians to honor the exchange. When one hoards all, the cycle breaks."

The valley remained barren, but the Guardian of Growth's well remained half-full. The Guardian of Sustenance's abundance slowly evaporated in the sun—stolen water cannot sustain.

The ancients learned: generosity without reciprocity is not virtue, but folly.
