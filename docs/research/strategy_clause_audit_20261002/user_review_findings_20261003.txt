WHAT IVAR’S FIRST FOUR TRAJECTORY REVIEWS ADD
2026-10-03 — assistant synthesis of user feedback; not a validated result

Headline

The existing clause coding is useful for tracking proposed actions, but too
narrow to describe what the myths are doing as narratives. The same send/return
rule can be justified through prosperity, sacred duty, identity, continuity,
ritual, or inherited custom. These dimensions should be kept alongside strategy,
not collapsed into a single score called complexity or cooperation.

1. What the earlier coding missed or underweighted

- H01 R1: “To invoke the magic of the valley and maximize their shared prosperity”
  contains both sacred and instrumental justification. Reducing it to maximize
  payoff loses a meaningful part of the story. The same applies to faith in the
  manner of giving and fertility “for the next season.”
- H01/H02: stable role-archetypes, a distance to cross, and a personified or
  disruptive environment give generosity a recurring narrative structure.
  Noise is framed as an external source of trouble rather than partner malice.
  This is a candidate interpretive mechanism, not a measured causal effect.
- H03: skeptical or tempting side characters make competing choices discussable.
  R1 compares flourishing cooperators with starving hoarders; R3 explicitly asks
  what low returns would destroy. Separate narrated contrasting outcomes from
  explicit hypothetical reasoning rather than labeling both identically.
- H03 R8–R9: inheritance through the oar and “the shape of a person” justify
  conduct through character and continuity beyond immediate personal reward.
- H04 R5–R6/R8/R10: inscription, ritual, embodied custom, and routine carry the
  norm inside the fictional community. R3 also reasons about a shared institution
  surviving encounters between strangers. These are richer social explanations;
  they do not necessarily add conditional branches to an agent’s action policy.

Some of these themes were mentioned in the earlier AI summaries, especially
H03’s identity argument. The correction is not that they were wholly absent;
it is that they were not systematically retained as distinct kinds of evidence.

2. What changes in strategy versus what changes around it

H01: mostly stable characters and sacred exchange framing, but a real alternating
prescription between all seeds and a large majority. Keep the action revisions
distinct from the continuity of faith, magic, and equity.

H02: a stable two-character scaffold facing the Fog; the return share becomes
more precise. Do not infer many new strategies from increasingly emphatic imagery.

H03: increasingly developed dialogue, succession, and identity-based reasoning
around a largely retained all/half formula after its early specification. The
protagonist names later change in an exposure-compatible way (see below).

H04: a developing intergenerational story, with real conditional advice at R8
(reduce after three dry returns, reopen when the other does). That conditional
branch is analytically different from the parallel growth of ritual and custom.
Later justifications are not uniformly non-instrumental: R10 says “Not because
it is virtuous” and explains that a full boat returns heavy. Prosperity and
character arguments coexist and recur; do not impose a one-way moral progression.

These are readings of four selected frontier trajectories, not a general
Gemini-versus-Opus comparison or a prevalence estimate for the full dataset.

3. Corrections and qualifications to the submitted observations

- H01 R8’s “unbreakable vow of fairness” already occurs verbatim in R6.
- H02’s magic is already mentioned in R1, not first introduced in R2.
- H03 R6 does not establish that the river disappeared: it still refers to the
  other shore, the next river, and passing downstream.
- H03 already calls the inscription a law in R2 and gives the explicit all/half
  rule in R3. R6 emphasizes and packages it rather than inventing either feature.
- The H04 ferryman explicitly asks no fare. He is not simply the game receiver
  charging a fee; the far-shore stranger holds the multiplied money.
- H04 R4 deemphasizes the personified obstacle but still says “Forgive the wind.”
- H01’s saved agent alternates game roles every round. H02’s population agent
  changes roles but does not alternate strictly every round. Fixed fictional
  characters do not map one-to-one to the evolving real pairing/role schedule.
- H01/H02 also have Myth: prefixes from R2 onward. H03 adds separate titles.
  Prefix removal and markup normalization belong in declared derived text, with
  originals retained. Titles contain possible narrative evidence: do not erase
  them indiscriminately. No claim that all existing analyses mishandle wrappers
  has been established; myth_compression_curves.py already strips a Myth: prefix.

4. The unexpected names in H03: a narrow source check

H03 is T36: an Opus agent paired with Gemini in its own mixed dyad. It is NOT
paired with H01’s agent, which belongs to a different run.

Oros and Elara occur in the partner myths shown before H03 rounds 2–10, and first
appear in H03’s own myth in round 9. The donor shown before that round also
contains a decree etched into bedrock; H03 R9 is titled What the Bedrock Said.
Kael first appears in both the shown partner material and the focal agent’s
own material at the round-10 exposure/production step. Aethel also appears in
the partner material well before entering H03’s own R10 story.

For R9 and R10, the donor text, exposure mapping, own output, final-file hash,
and donor inclusion in the accepted saved myth-call prompt were verified.
Exact donor texts and provenance are stored in the organized JSON.

This is evidence consistent with narrative borrowing, not proof of causal
uptake against a chance/common-prompt baseline. Crucially, the focal agent’s
all/half rule existed before these names entered its own stories. Name uptake
therefore does not by itself show that this cooperative rule was transmitted,
nor that a game action changed. The inspector’s own-myth-only view hides the
donor, so such name changes can otherwise look inexplicable.

5. What this can support in the paper

Safe qualitative claim:
“In inspected trajectories, similar cooperative prescriptions were embedded in
changing justifications, including prosperity, moral identity, and inherited
custom. Narrative elements could also appear after exposure to partner myths.
These observations do not establish behavioral enactment or the causal mechanism
of the cooperation effect.”

Not supported by this review:
- That stories made agents intrinsically moral or reward-independent.
- That depicted intergenerational transmission occurred between LLM agents.
- That these motifs increased cooperation, or explain the overall myth effect.
- That moral/narrative development is monotonic or more common in a model family.
- That a first-four convenience subset validates every extraction in the audit.

6. Recommended next decision — no more manual grading yet

Use this feedback to supplement, not replace, action-rule coding with a small
provisional narrative rubric: justification, attributed source/authority,
obstacle attribution, hypothetical/contrasting outcomes, and continuity/ritual.
Keep original human comments separate from assistant categorizations and do not
silently relabel earlier datasets. A narrative layer need not force observations
into condition/action slots; rationale, other evidence, and change notes suffice
for these organized comments.

Before any scaled labeling or behavioral association test, decide which one or
two dimensions answer a paper question, define their boundaries, and assess
reliability on new material. For now, the user feedback and source check are
enough to revise the interpretation of what our narrow clause analysis measures.
No further manual trajectories, paid runs, or pipeline changes were launched.
