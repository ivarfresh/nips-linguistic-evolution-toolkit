STEPS 4–5 FOLLOW-UP — 3 OCTOBER 2026

Bottom line
The bounded follow-up supports textual borrowing and plausible uptake of some
specific advice. It does not establish that borrowed advice changes play, or
that transmission explains the cooperation benefit. “Nothing transmits” is too
strong; “cooperative norms spread and thereby increase cooperation” is also too
strong. These are exploratory findings, not a replacement for existing tests.

What was completed
Checked the fixed 48 trajectories from 45 completed runs: 480 own myths and game
contexts, 432 actual exposures in accepted saved prompts, and 432 matched unseen
myths against their final files. Audited all 44 previously selected change events,
with all earlier own texts, peer and unseen comparisons, and preceding games.
Saved both subsequent role-specific decisions and their actual decision prompts;
later decisions can follow intervening myths and are not isolated effect windows.
No new simulation, paid model call, experimental change, or enlarged sample.

1. What appears to travel?
Across the later myths, new five-word sequences overlap the actually shown myth
more than the matched unseen myth. “New” here means absent from all earlier own
myths, not a new idea. The run-weighted exposed-minus-unseen difference is 1.08
percentage points (SD 2.61; descriptive run-bootstrap 95% interval 0.43–1.89;
45 runs). One later myth contains no novel sequences and contributes no fraction.
This is a modest lexical signal, not a norm measure, causal estimate or estimate
for all runs. Shared game experience and the selected sample limit interpretation.
The point estimate is positive in both cohorts and both task orders; this does
not establish a separate effect within every subgroup.

Some clause examples go beyond names or scenery:
- T40 R7 adds “when one arrives already cheated, overpay.” Its shown partner myth
  has “if one comes to you already cheated, overpay.” The original reading found
  this absent from own R1–R6 and the matched unseen myth; own R8/R9 retain it.
  This is a strong candidate for textual uptake of a compensation recommendation,
  not demonstrated compensation in the game.
- T34 R6 adds withdrawing slowly and sorrowfully while leaving the door open,
  closely matching its actual exposure. Earlier own text already used a season
  timescale; the candidate addition is the withdrawal procedure, not conditional
  thinking from scratch. The short after-quote has ZERO new exact five-word
  matches to the exposure: literal matching alone misses this paraphrase.
- T37 R8 combines three dry responses, reduced giving and immediate reopening.
  Actual exposure supplies reduce/reopen language, but the unseen comparison
  independently contains a similar forgiveness-count rule. Source attribution
  is therefore ambiguous, not a clean exposure-specific success.

The inherited machine classifications are 16 exposure candidates, 13 common
rules, 11 own continuations and 4 game-experience candidates. These are selected
examples, not independent validated labels, proportions of myths, or mutually
exclusive causal mechanisms. The new phrase checks supplement those readings;
they do not independently re-establish their semantic validity.

2. Do these changes predict action?
The frozen follow-up screened three bounded questions. None supports pooled
behavioral inference in this packet:
- Six selected forgiveness candidates: none supplies all the strict, measurable
  antecedents needed. “Repaid poorly” can prescribe a clear response while still
  leaving the numerical trigger unspecified. This is not six behavioral failures.
- Ten selected repeated-defection/reduction candidates: none passes the strict
  rule-definition gate. “Dry,” “season,” intention and partner applicability are
  not interchangeable with a numerical return threshold or round count.
- Six explicit sender-amount candidates: four have no sender decision before the
  next own myth. Two do: T21 R4 names three and sends 3 before and 3 afterwards
  (game rounds 4 and 5); T24 R4 names three and sends 3 before and 3 afterwards
  (previous sender round 1 and current round 4). Both already enacted the amount.

The narrow pre-next-myth window protects attribution to the selected text; it
does not mean later behavior is irrelevant or that rules cannot persist. Two
cases are below the frozen ten-run gate and show no change to explain. No pooled
regression, significance test, conditional null effect, or behavioral effect size
was manufactured from these exclusions. Trigger ambiguity and few opportunities
are identification limits, not evidence that advice is ignored.

3. What this changes in our interpretation
Separate three claims: agents borrow language; some borrowed language is advice;
that advice changes behavior. The packet supports the first and supplies credible
examples for the second, but does not establish the third. Exact rule endpoints
can miss paraphrase and recombination. Conversely, broader semantic labels alone
cannot solve timing, ceiling behavior, vague triggers or competing game experience.
Game-before-myth cases especially permit behavior to explain the new story.

For the paper, the defensible exploratory statement is:
“Trajectory inspection identified candidate uptake and adaptation of specific
partner advice, including compensation and conditional withdrawal. However,
these cases did not provide sufficient temporally isolated behavioral evidence
to show that advice uptake caused changes in cooperation.”
Do not present this as established human-validated coding. Ivar's four reviewed
packet trajectories informed interpretation, but reliability remains unmeasured.
This follow-up was fixed before its new numerical summaries, not preregistered
or blind to all earlier game examples. It is not a census of every rule change.
