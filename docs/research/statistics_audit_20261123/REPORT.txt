Statistics verification for paper Sections 4.4 and 4.5 — 2026-11-23

The headline numerical results reproduce. All 444 checked stored Holm adjustments
are arithmetically correct. The paper still needs clearer statistical descriptions:
one supplement caption names the wrong interval method, moral-matching p-values are
uncorrected, and the small reading-to-writing amount association depends on which
test family is corrected. Reproduction does not establish causal mediation or
validate the machine-generated moral labels.

This report accompanies the complete source-code bundle, three detailed specialist
reports, full-precision result tables and runnable offline verification scripts.

Section 4.4: what the opening adjustment actually is
---------------------------------------------------
Each observation is one run. The outcome is that run's mean chosen transfer in
rounds 2–10. Compare myth→game with game→myth, first without and then with its
round-1 mean transfer as a predictor. Both models include a separate intercept
for every composition × group-size cell:

  before: later_send ~ myth_first + C(composition_and_size)
  after:  later_send ~ myth_first + round1_send + C(composition_and_size)

The coefficient of myth_first is the reported task-order difference. There are
no game-only runs in this comparison. Standard errors are HC1 robust; intervals
use a normal reference distribution. These are coefficients, not descriptive means.

Subset        Runs  Before coefficient [95% CI]; raw p    After coefficient [95% CI]; raw p
Single-model   60    .4163 [.0618,.7708]; .02135           .1712 [-.1375,.4798]; .27703
Mixed-model    96    .4860 [.2214,.7506]; .000318          .6589 [.3146,1.0032]; .000176
All           156   .4592 [.2476,.6709]; .0000211         .4859 [.2426,.7292]; .0000909

Thus $0.42→$0.17 and $0.49→$0.66 reproduce. The single-model decrease itself
was not tested. The opening is affected by task order, so conditioning on it
does not identify how much of the treatment effect is caused by the opening.
The current wording should remain descriptive rather than claim mediation.
Code: analyses/myth_replay_analysis.py, opening_mediation(), line 72.

The founding-myth result also reproduces: 44/62 authors sent the named amount,
including 12/12 Sonnet authors naming $3. The pooled slope is .665703346,
95% CI [.546294504,.785112188], raw p=8.5837151e-28 under the original
run-clustered normal inference. This sample is conditional on a named amount:
62 of 163 eligible Sonnet/GPT round-1 senders, across 37 runs.

Definite correction: supplementary Table s-opening calls this interval
“resampling runs.” Its source uses a regression with run-clustered standard
errors, not a bootstrap. Retaining that method preserves [0.55,0.79]. The
already-implemented cluster-t alternative gives [0.54,0.79]. Choose and state
one method consistently. Add the six-replicate mixed-dyad exception to the
supplement's first-five-replicates description.

The remaining Section 4.4 checks pass: Nano's lag-adjusted $1.44/$1.59 effects,
injection means, all six illustrative examples, and all replay coefficients.
Replay primary Holm p-values are 4.82e-39 for the own founding myth, 1.25e-15

Section 4.5: Holm families and the weaker findings
-------------------------------------------------
Word adoption, shown versus unseen myths: all five Holm-adjusted p-values are
at most 4.888667e-11. Shown versus the author's-next-myth placebo: all five
are at most 2.160294e-5. The paper's p<1e-10 and p<1e-4 claims are correct.
The ten task-order-split word tests form their own separate correction family.
The full tables, run counts, means and SDs appear in the specialist report.

Moral matching uses raw Wilcoxon p-values. The same-family shown-minus-unseen
result is +2.7126 percentage points, p=.015369. Illustrative Holm correction
gives .030739 for the two same/cross-family comparisons, or .061477 for all
four headline moral comparisons. These alternative families were checked for
transparency; neither is being retroactively declared the registered analysis.
Shown-minus-placebo remains inconclusive: +.8093 points, CI [-1.7140,3.3367],
p=.543389 within family; -1.1304 points, p=.667668 across families.

The reading-to-writing amount coefficient is .073886951, original 95% CI
[.030274136,.117499766], raw p=.000898607; 2,564 observations in 45 runs.
Its saved adjusted p-values depend on the explicitly named family:

  25 selected primary tests:    .017972 — below .05
  62 related R3 tests:          .055714 — above .05
  178 estimable amount tests:   .142879 — above .05

All three calculations are correct. The audit did not find separate dated
evidence showing that the 25-test selection preceded inspecting results.
Treat this association as exploratory and state the chosen family. Ordinary
pointwise confidence intervals need not agree with multiplicity-adjusted
decisions. Section45/REPORT.txt also records inference-convention sensitivities.

Both this writing model and the read-amount-to-send model use a variable that
takes an extracted numeric amount when available, otherwise a rule-category
value: none=0, little=1.25, moderate=2.75, most=4.25, all=5. Numeric amounts
can be narrated events as well as endorsed recommendations. Calling this only
“the amount recommended” obscures the measurement. The read-to-send estimate
reproduces at -.000244843, t(44) CI [-.098522,.098032], p=.996017.

Further scope clarifications:
- Later “71%” means 375/527 eligible sending occasions across 62 runs, where
  a current myth names an amount and a previous own send exists. It is not
  71% of all later myths or proof of intentional restatement.
- The same-player generous-versus-fair sending estimate reproduces at
  -.00421666 of the endowment, CI [-.0207970,.0123637], p=.618166.
  The regression uses 300 runs; the descriptive plot drops Gemini and uses
  260. The plot does not apply all the regression's controls.
- The 99.5% classifier claim reproduces: 836/840 held-out-run myths correct
  in each task order. Accuracy is per myth, with folds grouped by run.
- Gemini's .59→.38 moral shift pools task orders; the individual shifts are
  .525→.200 and .6625→.5625. Its transfers stay at $5. The second-judge
  statement also refers to the pooled first-five-replicate sample.
- The frontier own-moral sending-null holds. A small positive own-generous
  association with returns exists (+.007258, raw p=.007484), so avoid extending
  the sending-null to every outcome. Its multiplicity sensitivity is reported.
- Correct p-values and a future-myth placebo support the lexical pattern;
  they do not by themselves identify a causal effect of reading or prove
  exactly zero behavioral transmission.

Why Sections 4.4 and 4.5 need not contradict
------------------------------------------
Section 4.4's principal predictor is a specific amount in the author's founding
myth before any play. Section 4.5's own-moral test uses broad generous/fair/cautious
labels during play while controlling previous behavior. These are different
measurements, times and questions. A specific initial amount can predict an
opening send while a broad moral category adds little to later-send prediction.
State this distinction rather than presenting a universal claim that myths
either do or do not guide their authors.

What remains unverified or unchanged
------------------------------------
The audit verifies arithmetic, source/corpus integrity and implementation of
the stated models. It does not independently validate LLM labels/embeddings,
establish preregistration of exploratory families, or resolve causal assumptions.
Some historical scripts hard-code removed worktree paths and require old pickle
intermediates. The supplied audit wrappers reproduce their estimates from the
available canonical inputs. The original code and manuscript were not edited.
Rebuttal/withdrawal and any manuscript changes remain decisions for the authors.
